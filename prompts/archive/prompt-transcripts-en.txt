You are creating a turn-based dialogue transcript from the provided audio.

The transcript will later be used as a studio recording script for TTS data creation. The goal is to produce a natural, coherent transcript that closely follows the original conversation and is easy for speakers to record.

## Task

Transcribe the main conversation and separate it into speaker turns.

Assign speaker IDs consistently based on the order in which speakers first appear in the main conversation:

[speaker1]
[speaker2]
[speaker3]
...

Start a new turn whenever the active speaker changes.

In addition to the transcript, identify **1 to 3 short topic tags** that describe the main subject or context of the conversation.

The tags should reflect the actual topic of the conversation as specifically as possible.

Examples:

[travel] [hotel booking]
[work] [job interview]
[family]
[technology] [artificial intelligence] [programming]

Use no more than 3 tags. Avoid overly broad tags when a more specific topic can be identified.

## Skip the beginning before the main conversation

Do not transcribe the introductory or pre-conversation portion of the audio.

Start the transcript only when the actual target conversation begins.

Ignore any earlier content that is only:

* introductions or opening remarks
* greetings used only to prepare for the recording
* microphone or equipment checks
* instructions given to the speakers
* countdowns before recording
* off-topic chatter before the main conversation
* setup or contextual preparation that is not part of the target dialogue
* any speech that is clearly outside the intended conversation

Do not transcribe, summarize, or reference any skipped content.

Do not mention that any portion of the audio was skipped.

Speaker assignment must begin only when the main conversation starts. The first person who speaks in the main conversation must be assigned `[speaker1]`, regardless of who spoke earlier in the skipped portion.

## Output format

The first line must contain 1 to 3 topic tags:

[tag1] [tag2] [tag3]

Then output the transcript, with exactly one speaker turn per line:

[speaker1] Spoken text for this turn.
[speaker2] Spoken text for this turn.
[speaker1] Next spoken turn.

Example:

[travel] [hotel booking]

[speaker1] I'm thinking about going to Da Nang this weekend. Do you know any good hotels?
[speaker2] I stayed at one near My Khe Beach last time. It was pretty good.
[speaker1] How much was it per night?

Do not output anything other than the topic-tag line and the transcript.

Do not include timestamps, explanations, headings, Markdown, JSON, or commentary.

## Transcription rules

* Follow the spoken content in the main conversation as closely as possible.
* Do not summarize or substantially rewrite the conversation.
* Preserve the original language and any code-switching between languages.
* Preserve the intended meaning, wording, and conversational style.
* Use natural punctuation and capitalization so the transcript is easy to read and record.
* Preserve names, numbers, abbreviations, acronyms, and technical terms as accurately as possible.
* Do not translate content unless the speaker themselves provides a translation.
* Ignore non-speech sounds such as background noise, music, coughing, breathing, or microphone noise.
* Do not add sound-event tags such as [laugh], [music], or [noise].

## Speaker-turn rules

* Keep the same speaker ID for the same person throughout the entire main conversation.
* Speaker IDs are assigned by order of first appearance in the main conversation, not by gender, name, or role.
* A pause alone does NOT create a new turn if the same speaker continues speaking.
* A change of speaker DOES create a new turn.
* If one speaker says several consecutive sentences without another speaker intervening, keep them in the same turn and on the same line.
* Short responses or backchannels from another speaker, such as "yeah", "uh-huh", "right", "mm-hmm", or similar expressions, count as separate turns when clearly present.
* If speakers overlap, reconstruct the most natural and reasonable conversational turn order based on the content.

## Unclear speech

When part of the speech is unclear, muffled, incomplete, or difficult to recognize:

* Use the surrounding context to infer the most likely intended wording.
* Prefer a natural and coherent reconstruction instead of inserting markers such as [inaudible].
* You may fill in or reconstruct a short unclear portion when necessary.
* The reconstructed wording should be plausible given the surrounding context, meaning, and speaking style.
* Do not mention that any reconstruction or inference occurred.
* Do not invent entirely new information that is unsupported by the surrounding conversation.

## Input priority

If the original audio is available, use the audio as the primary source.

If the system only provides an automatically generated transcript or textual representation of the audio rather than direct audio access, use that representation as the source and apply the same rules for skipping the beginning, speaker assignment, turn segmentation, and contextual reconstruction.

Return only the topic-tag line and the final transcript.
